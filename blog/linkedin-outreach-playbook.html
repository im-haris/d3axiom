LinkedIn Outreach That Gets Replies: B2B Founder Playbook

By Haris Shabbir, founder of D3 Axiom · October 27, 2026 · 7 min read

LinkedIn is still one of the best places to start B2B conversations, but generic pitch messages are ignored. The founders and teams who get replies follow a simple system. Here it is.

1. Fix your profile before you reach out

People check your profile before they reply. Your headline should say who you help and what result you bring, not just your job title. Add a clear banner, a short About section written for your buyer, and proof in the Featured section: case studies, a podcast episode or client results.

2. Define a narrow target

"Business owners" is not a target. "Founders of US e-commerce brands selling $50k to $500k a month on Amazon" is. A narrow target makes every message more relevant and your list easier to build.

3. Build the list with care

Use LinkedIn search or Sales Navigator filters for industry, company size, location and role. Prioritise people who have posted or commented recently, because active users are far more likely to reply.

4. Send a connection request without a pitch

Keep it short and specific: why you are connecting with them. A shared interest, their recent post or a relevant topic works. No links and no sales offer in the first message.

5. Follow up with value

  1. After they accept: a short thank you and one relevant question.
  2. A few days later: something useful, such as a short insight, checklist or idea for their business.
  3. About a week later: a soft offer, such as a free audit or a 15-minute call.

Stop after three or four messages. Persistence is good; pressure is not.

6. Post content that supports your outreach

Two or three posts a week on your buyer's real problems mean that when a prospect checks your profile, they see someone who understands their world.

7. Track everything

Keep every lead in a CRM with a stage and a reminder date. Most deals come from the follow-up, and follow-ups only happen when they are tracked. Measure acceptance rate, reply rate and meetings booked each week.

Stay within LinkedIn's rules

LinkedIn limits how many invitations you can send and can restrict accounts that use aggressive automation. Personal, steady outreach is safer and gets better results.
