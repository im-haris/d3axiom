How to Find and Fix Bleeding Keywords in Amazon PPC

By Haris Shabbir, founder of D3 Axiom · October 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Every Amazon ad account has bleeders: search terms and targets that keep spending money but bring back few or no sales. They rarely look dramatic on their own. Together they are often the biggest reason ACoS sits higher than it should. Here is a simple, repeatable way to find and fix them.

1. Know your break-even ACoS first

You cannot call a keyword "bad" without a target. Your break-even ACoS equals your profit margin before ad spend. If a product sells for $30 and leaves $9 profit before ads, break-even ACoS is 30%. Anything above that loses money on each ad sale; anything below it is profitable.

2. Pull the right report

Download the Sponsored Products search term report for the last 30 to 60 days. Leave out the most recent few days, because recent clicks can still turn into orders and make good terms look bad.

3. Use clear rules to flag bleeders

  • No sales after enough clicks. If your product converts at about 10%, a term with 20 or more clicks and zero orders is very likely a bleeder.
  • Spend above your target cost per sale. If a term has spent more than you are willing to pay for one order and has not produced one, flag it.
  • ACoS far above break-even with enough orders to trust the number.

4. Pick the right fix for each term

  • Irrelevant term (wrong product, wrong intent): add it as a negative exact, or negative phrase if a whole family of searches is wrong.
  • Relevant term, too expensive: lower the bid towards your target instead of blocking it.
  • Relevant term, many clicks, no orders: the problem may be the listing, price or reviews, not the keyword. Check the product page before you cut traffic.

5. Do not over-correct

Low-data terms with only a few clicks need more time. Branded and competitor terms may have a different goal. Make changes in small steps, note what you changed, and review the effect after two weeks.

Make it a weekly routine

Bleeders come back as Amazon finds new search terms for your ads. A short weekly check of new terms with spend and no sales keeps waste from building up again. For larger accounts, this is exactly the kind of work worth automating with clear rules and an approval step.
